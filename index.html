CrossSum — Privacy Policy

App: CrossSum — Math Crossword Puzzle (app.crosssum.game)

Last updated: 1 October 2026

CrossSum is a puzzle game that runs on your phone. This policy says what it stores, what leaves the

phone, and what does not. It is short because the game itself does very little with your data: there is

no account, no sign-in, no sync, and no server of ours anywhere in it.

What the game stores on your phone

One small file: which levels you have finished, the stars you earned, whether you turned the sound off,

whether you allowed personalised ads, and the board you were in the middle of. It stays on your phone,

it is not backed up to us, and there is nobody to send it to. Uninstalling the app deletes it, and so

does the Delete saved game control on the game's own Privacy screen.

What the advertisements do

CrossSum shows ads supplied by Google through the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob), and only between puzzles — never inside

one. To serve those ads, and for its own analytics and fraud prevention, that SDK collects and shares

with Google:

  • your device's IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of the device;
  • your interactions with the app and the ads — for example app launches, taps and video views;
  • diagnostic information about how the app performs, such as launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device identifiers, including the Android advertising id and the app set id.

All of it is encrypted in transit. It is Google's data, collected by Google's software under Google's

own policy, which is worth reading and which also explains your controls:

https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the game asks for your consent

before it shows personalised ads, through Google's own consent platform, and shows no ads at all

until that question is answered — a refusal costs you nothing but the ads. Google keeps that answer; the

game only reads whether it was given.

You can also reset or delete your advertising id — and turn off ad personalisation for your whole

device — in Android's own settings (usually Settings → Security and privacy → Ads). Doing that does not

break this game: the puzzles are inside the app and do not need any of it.

The developer of CrossSum does not collect any of this. It is not sent to us, we cannot see it, and

there is nothing in this app that could identify you to us.

What is never collected

No name, no email address, no account, no phone number, no contacts, no photos or files, no precise

location, no microphone and no camera. The game itself needs no network at all: every level is inside

the app, and if the phone is offline the only thing that changes is that no ad appears.

Children

CrossSum is a puzzle game for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. It has no

account, no chat and no user-generated content, and the advertising it shows is not configured for

child-directed treatment: the app is explicitly not tagged as child-directed, because that is a

statement about who the app is for rather than a setting about a particular player, and this game is for

teenagers and adults. If you believe a child has used the app and you want to know what the ad SDK

did with their device's advertising id, the controls above apply to that device: the id can be reset or

deleted, and ad personalisation turned off, in Android's settings.

Your choices

Everything a player can decide about their own data lives on one screen in the game, under Privacy,

and these are those choices in the same words the screen uses (D-055):

  • Personalised ads: turn personalised ads off in the game itself, and every ad it asks for from that

moment is asked for without personalisation.

  • Ad privacy choices: change your mind about ad consent from inside the game, wherever the law

requires the game to ask. Where the law does not, the game has nothing to show you and says so.

  • Delete saved game: delete everything the game has stored on the phone, without uninstalling it —

the levels, their stars, the board in flight, and the ad setting above.

There is nothing to ask us to delete, because we have no server and no profile of you. If you write to

us anyway, we will answer. And two things are Android's rather than the game's, so no game can do them

for you: resetting or deleting the advertising id, and turning off ad personalisation for the whole

device. Both live in Android's own settings (usually Settings → Security and privacy → Ads).

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the new version is published at the same

URL. Nothing about the app's data behaviour has changed since the first release: the app has always kept

its saved game on the phone and has never had an account.
